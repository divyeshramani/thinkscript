## 3. Core Philosophy

### 3.1 No Indicators — Price, Volume, and Patterns Only

> *"I use absolutely no indicators whatsoever. I simply rely on chart patterns, price, and volume."* — Dan Zanger (S&C Magazine Interview, 2003)

> *"They look pretty on the charts, but for the most part are very ineffective for my use. This is especially true in powerful markets that are up or down."*

> *"Trading for me is all about volume and price action."*

### 3.2 Momentum / Consolidation Breakout Strategy

> *"In theory Dan looked for a consolidation in price and would trade the volume through a line of resistance. In essence a consolidation breakout strategy."*

> *"If a stock doesn't accelerate quickly out of a basing area, then I'll sell the stock promptly during that first day. I'll do this even if I've been in the stock for only 20 minutes, regardless of profit or loss at that time. If a stock isn't moving up sharply right away, then the trade must be wrong."*

> *"Remember stocks are only good when they are moving up."*

> *"Stocks are my buddies, I know when they feel good and when they feel bad."*

### 3.3 Key Principles

- **Buy strength, not hope for reversals**
- **Cut losses fast — never stay wrong**
- **Let winners run — scale out gradually**
- **Never predict — react to price and volume signals**
- **No margin until mastery is achieved**
- **Trade with the overall market trend**

## 5. CANSLIM Fundamental Framework

Zanger combined William O'Neil's CANSLIM methodology with his own chart pattern expertise:

**C — Current Quarterly Earnings Growth:**
- Quarterly EPS growth >20% vs. year-ago quarter
- 20% EPS growth from prior quarter

**A — Annual Earnings Growth:**
- Annual EPS growth >20%

**N — New Products, New Management, New Highs**

**S — Supply and Demand:**
- Smaller float preferred; high demand relative to supply

**L — Leader or Laggard:**
- Trade leaders only; avoid lagging stocks in weak sectors

**I — Institutional Sponsorship:**
- Look for institutional buying as a confirmation signal

**M — Market Direction:**
- Trade with the overall market trend; in bear markets, go to cash

### Fundamental Screening Requirements
- **Earnings Growth:** Quarterly EPS >20% growth, Annual EPS >20% growth
- **Revenue Growth:** 20% increase in sales over recent quarter
- **Purpose:** These metrics align with what large institutions (banks, funds) look for, putting the trader on the side of "big money"

## 6. Chart Patterns Traded

### 6.1 Primary Patterns

| Pattern | Description | Key Characteristics |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Consolidation Breakout** | Price consolidates, then breaks above resistance with volume | Most common pattern; appears across all timeframes |
| **Bull Flag** | Brief consolidation (parallel channels) after a strong move, then breakout | 7-day consolidation period typical; continuation pattern |
| **Cup and Handle** | Rounded bottom (cup) followed by brief handle consolidation | Classic breakout setup; big winners during bubble |
| **Ascending Triangle** | Rising lows converging with flat resistance | Contraction in volatility; breakout above resistance |
| **Symmetrical Triangle** | Converging trendlines (lower highs, higher lows) | Contraction followed by directional breakout |
| **Descending Triangle** | Flat support with descending highs | Breakout above resistance |
| **Flat Base Pattern** | Horizontal consolidation followed by breakout | Clean resistance line; volume confirms breakout |
| **High-Level Symmetrical Triangle** | Triangle forming at elevated price levels | Often occurs after strong initial move |
| **Small Descending Trendline** | Short-term downtrend line within a base | Break above signals entry |

### 6.2 Pattern Commonalities

**All Dan Zanger patterns share these traits:**
1. **Consolidation** — Price compresses into a defined range
2. **Volume increases** — Volume must confirm at the breakout point
3. **Lines of resistance** — Clear resistance line is identified
4. **Breakout of resistance** — Entry occurs on the breakout above resistance

## 7. Volume Analysis

> *"Volume is everything. No stock can make a move up without volume. The greater the volume, the better the potential move."* — Dan Zanger

### 7.1 Volume Rules

- **Primary Volume Metric:** 20-day average volume as baseline
- **Intraday Volume Monitoring:** Breaks trading day into 13 periods; monitors volume in each period for buying/selling pressure
- **Breakout Confirmation:** Volume must increase at the point of resistance breakout
- **Failure Signal:** If price rises but volume does not follow → suspect setup → exit

### 7.2 Volume Interpretation

- Rising volume = strong institutional participation = valid breakout
- Declining volume on price rise = warning sign
- Volume spike on breakout = highest conviction entry signal
- Zanger described volume + price as a "pressure factor" that tells him what the stock wants to do next and when

## 8. Entry & Exit Rules

### 8.1 Entry Rules

1. **Pattern must be well-formed** — Clear base/consolidation visible on chart
2. **Volume must confirm** — Rising volume at or before breakout
3. **Price must break resistance** — Entry on breakout above the trendline/resistance
4. **Buy price discipline** — Never pay more than 5% above the breakout point
5. **Industry group strength** — Stock should be in a strong sector/industry group
6. **Fundamental confirmation** — Earnings and revenue growth meet CANSLIM criteria (>20%)
7. **Market alignment** — Overall market must be favorable (not in confirmed downtrend)
8. **Watch action on break** — Observe quality of price action before committing full position

### 8.2 Exit Rules

| Condition | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Price drops below breakout point | **Sell immediately** — 5–7% stop loss |
| Stock doesn't accelerate quickly | **Sell same day**, even if held only 20 minutes |
| Stock gains 15–20% from breakout | **Sell 20–30% of position** (scale out) |
| Reversal patterns appear (candlestick) | **Sell entire position** |
| Stock loses upward momentum | **Sell immediately** — "Stocks are only good when moving up" |
| Volume stops rising | **Get out** — Volume is the fuel |
| Market turns bearish | **Go to cash entirely** |
| Stock fails to act favorably | **Sell and rotate** to stronger setups |

### 8.3 Exit Philosophy

- **No trailing stops** — Uses mental stops and discretionary judgment
- **"Be quick to sell the stock should the price drop below the breakout point"** — Dan alludes to 5–7% stop loss
- **"I never use trailing stops, but I will sell a position of strong stocks moving up quickly or for long periods of time"**
- **Sells on first considerable sign of momentum change**
- **Studies losing trades afterward** to identify what was missed

## 9. Risk Management & Position Sizing

### 9.1 Stop Loss Rules

- **Hard stop: 5% below entry** (some sources say 5–7%)
- **Failed breakout = immediate exit**, regardless of profit/loss
- **Mental stops** rather than automated stop-loss orders (requires extreme discipline)
- **"Be quick to sell"** is the governing principle — hesitation is the enemy

### 9.2 Position Sizing

- **Scale out at 15–20% gain:** Sell 20–30% of position
- **Hold remaining position** as long as momentum continues
- **Never risk more than can be afforded to lose** on any single trade
- **Uses margin carefully** — only after mastering emotions and charts
- At Westwood Capital ($40M hedge fund): traded 300,000–800,000 shares/day in ~6 stocks; held 4 days to 2 weeks (max 30 days)

### 9.3 Risk Philosophy

> *"Never go on margin until you have mastered the market, charts and your emotions. Margin can wipe you out."*

> *"Long or short, you lose. The market whipsawed so heavily, it chewed up strategies."* (Learned from 2001–2002 bear market — went to cash entirely)

- **Drawdowns experienced:** Lost 32% in one day (October 2000); lost 75% of portfolio during bear market
- **Lesson:** When market conditions deteriorate, abandon the strategy and go to cash entirely
- **Known for offensive rather than defensive trading** — high conviction, high risk/reward

- **Monitors volume in 13 periods** throughout the trading day
- Watches buying/selling pressure

### 10.3 Post-Market

- **Nightly newsletter** (The Zanger Report): publishes 3x/week (Sunday, Monday, Wednesday) with ~16 charts per issue covering:
  - Major indices analysis
  - Short-term sentiment via NYMO
  - Major trends and story stocks
  - Personal picks and setups being watched
  - Macro information
  - Chart analysis with highlighted patterns

### 10.4 Trading Frequency

- **Typical holding period:** 4–12 days (most trades); ranges from 20 minutes to 10 weeks
- **Never holds longer than 30 days**
- **Average:** ~4.92 weeks per trade
- **At Westwood Capital:** ~6 stocks at a time, 300K–800K shares/day
